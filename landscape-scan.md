# Online BME Master's: Combined Landscape Scan

Scan dates: first pass and second pass, September 2026. This document merges both. Each program claim carries a verification status. "Linked" means I read it on an official or named page and the link is given. "Second pass" means it came from the expanded research report, which reported official university pages as its source, but the link wasn't carried into this document; confirm before external use. "Inference" is my reasoning and is labeled. Where a figure wasn't available, the document says so.

## How to read the field

The online BME field splits into four groups, and the rest of this document follows them. Direct competitors are fully online or online-completable master's degrees labeled biomedical engineering or bioengineering. Finish-remotely and hybrid models let a campus student complete the master's online or require only short residencies. Adjacent programs compete for the same working engineer without the BME label, mainly regulatory affairs, device engineering, clinical engineering, and AI in medicine. Signals are programs announced, paused, or deliberately kept in person, which say something about where the market is going.

### Program detail

**University of Houston.** This is the most important addition from the second pass because it resets the price floor. The MS is fully online with no campus requirement and uses the same faculty as the campus program. The department states a flat $745 per credit with no added fees, so a 30-credit master's costs $22,350 and a 9-credit certificate costs $6,705. Admission generally expects three semesters of calculus, differential equations, linear algebra, and a STEM bachelor's, with provisional admission and leveling courses for broader STEM backgrounds. Enrollment isn't published. Status: second pass.

**Colorado State.** A 30-credit coursework MEng with core, foundation, depth, and breadth courses, offered on campus and online through the School of Biomedical Engineering. The $964 per credit figure comes from a CSU-sponsored post, so treat it as indicative. Status: second pass.

**Purdue.** Still the program closest to what BU would build. The 30 credits are two BME technical courses (biostatistics, signal processing, biosensors, imaging diagnostics, MRI theory, image processing, BioMEMS, biomaterials), one quantitative course, one life-sciences course including Introduction to Clinical Medicine, four courses from a regulatory and professional list, and two electives. That list includes Preclinical and Clinical Study Design, Regulatory Issues Surrounding Approvals of Biomedical Devices, Quality Systems for Regulatory Compliance, Ethical Engineering of Medical Tech, Entrepreneurship in BME, Human Factors of Medical Devices, and systems engineering courses. The sample plan has no capstone ([curriculum](https://engineering.purdue.edu/BME/Academics/Graduate/Online/degree-requirements-curriculum)). A regulatory affairs certificate stacks into the degree ([Purdue Online](https://www.purdue.edu/online/?p=7120)), and Purdue's showcase graduate moved into devices and regulatory affairs ([Purdue BME](https://engineering.purdue.edu/BME/Academics/Graduate/Online)). The interdisciplinary route can pair with Purdue's online MBA. Admissions requirements weren't read. Enrollment isn't published.

**Case Western.** Four BME courses, two core engineering courses, two translational courses, and two technical electives ([program page](https://case.edu/programs/biomedical-engineering-online)). The translational layer covers regulatory aspects of health technology, biodesign, engineering management, and team projects. Online courses use simulation software and team projects with campus students ([master's programs](https://case.edu/bme/academics/graduate/masters-programs)). A capstone ties the core to medical device design and innovation ([curriculum](https://online-engineering.case.edu/online-degrees/ms-biomedical-engineering/curriculum)). Admission expects a 3.0 GPA, calculus through differential equations, and three years of physical, chemical, and engineering sciences ([admissions](https://case.edu/programs/biomedical-engineering-ms)). Everspring supports delivery ([CWRU BME](https://case.edu/bme/academics/graduate/masters-programs/master-biomedical-engineering-online)). Admissions run through Spring 2027 per the second pass.

**Maryland.** A ten-course, non-thesis MEng with a residency-blind rate ([catalog](https://academiccatalog.umd.edu/graduate/programs/bioengineering-online-mebi/)). Admission requires a STEM bachelor's with a 3.0 and prerequisites ([MAGE](https://mage.umd.edu/programs/bioengineering)). The second pass found MAGE's online rate of $1,567 per credit against $1,271 for standard on-campus courses. A secondary directory lists courses in regulatory affairs, device development, physiology, image analysis, machine learning, and biosensors ([directory](https://www.onlineengineeringprograms.com/usa/maryland)), which I haven't confirmed on UMD's pages. MAGE told U.S. News that bioengineering is not among its five largest areas ([U.S. News](https://www.usnews.com/education/online-education/university-of-maryland-college-park-OENG0187/engineering)), which suggests it's small.

**Johns Hopkins EP.** Ten courses within five years, plus a residency that is mostly online but includes two lab weekends in Baltimore ([JHU Hub](https://hub.jhu.edu/2015/06/30/johns-hopkins-online-applied-biomedical-engineering-degree)). Courses include Physiology I and II, Molecular Biology, Mathematical Methods, Principles of Medical Instrumentation and Devices, Rehabilitation Engineering, and Regulation of Medical Devices ([planning list](https://ep.jhu.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/ABE-Course-Planning-List-2024-10-24-distro.pdf)), along with Cell and Tissue Engineering, x-ray and CT imaging, Frontiers in Neuroengineering, and Brain-Computer Interfaces ([catalog](https://e-catalogue.jhu.edu/course-descriptions/applied_biomedical_engineering/)). Focus areas cover imaging, medical devices, neuroengineering, and translational tissue engineering, plus a general area whose name was cut off in what I read ([program page](https://ep.jhu.edu/programs/applied-biomedical-engineering)). Provisional admission is allowed for missing prerequisites ([summary](https://www.mastersportal.com/studies/481407/applied-biomedical-engineering.html)). Faculty include Applied Physics Lab and Johns Hopkins Hospital people ([landing page](https://info.ep.jhu.edu/abe/)).

**USC Viterbi.** 28 units, with 17 required and at least 11 elective, and an optional 4-unit thesis ([USC Online](https://online.usc.edu/programs/biomedical-engineering-ms/)). Admission expects an engineering or related bachelor's, with no GRE for 2026 ([admissions](https://viterbigradadmission.usc.edu/programs/masters/msprograms/biomedical-engineering/ms-biomedical-engineering/)). What USC keeps off DEN matters more: its Medical Device and Diagnostic Engineering MS is explicitly not online ([MDDE](https://viterbigradadmission.usc.edu/programs/masters/msprograms/biomedical-engineering/ms-in-medical-device-and-diagnostic-engineering/)), and its imaging informatics MS is not currently available ([MIII](https://viterbigradadmission.usc.edu/programs/masters/msprograms/biomedical-engineering/ms-bme-medical-imaging-imaging-informatics/)).

**Columbia.** A required computational modeling of physiological systems course, two core courses (quantitative physiology, solid biomechanics, physiological control systems and similar), two BME electives (artificial organs, cardiac mechanics, diagnostic ultrasound, tissue engineering, computational genomics), one applied math course, and four free electives. No capstone or thesis. Admission requires a 3.0, three letters, and a year each of calculus, biology, chemistry, and physics, with the GRE optional ([CVN](https://cvn.columbia.edu/content/biomedical-engineering-masters-degree)). This is the most science-heavy and most expensive program in the set.

**Florida Atlantic.** The department marks the MS as available fully online. The campus version is 30 credits with a 6-credit defended thesis or a non-thesis option and electives across biological sciences, biomedical sciences, complex systems and brain sciences, and nursing. Online tuition isn't published. Status: second pass.

**Michigan.** This corrects the first pass, which found no online BME master's at Michigan. Michigan Engineering announced AI+ Engineering master's programs in July 2026 ([Michigan Engineering News](https://news.engin.umich.edu/2026/07/new-ai-engineering-masters-programs-and-certificate-launches-on-campus-and-online/)), and biomedical is one of seven concentrations ([BME announcement](https://bme.umich.edu/2026/07/20/u-m-bme-to-expand-ai-engineering-education-with-new-masters-pathway/)). The BME concentration is 30 credits: 12 in AI and ML foundations, 12 at the intersection with biomedicine, and 6 in knowledge extensions. All required courses but one (AI for Experiment Design) are available online ([program page](https://bme.umich.edu/academics/graduate/m-eng-certificate-program-overview/)). One timing conflict needs checking: the BME page gives a Fall 2027 MEng start and a Winter 2027 certificate, while the college release says remote options may start at the same time as the campus launch or later.

**Enrollment.** No direct competitor publishes online enrollment on the pages reviewed. Real numbers would need IPEDS completions by CIP code or direct inquiry, and I'm not confident IPEDS separates online from campus completions at the program level.

## 2. Finish-remotely and hybrid models

**Penn Engineering's Online Accelerated Master's (4+).** Your recollection is right about the mechanism and too broad about the scope. Qualified Penn undergraduates take up to three graduate courses during the bachelor's, then finish the remaining courses, typically seven, fully online from anywhere within three years at their own pace. It also serves students who started the campus 4+1 but relocated or took jobs, and campus 4+1 students can switch into it. Students apply in their final undergraduate semester. The limit is that it covers only Penn Engineering's existing online master's, which are computing and data-science degrees; bioengineering isn't among them. Status: second pass.

**Michigan's sequential degrees.** Michigan's SUGS program lets undergraduates double-count up to 9 credits toward a master's ([SUGS](https://bme.umich.edu/academics/graduate/masters/accelerated-masters/)). The second pass notes it can feed the online-capable AI+ BME MEng, which effectively creates a roll-up master's with an online finish once that program launches.

**Short-residency hybrids.** JHU's two lab weekends are the only working model found for keeping hands-on content in an online BME degree. Yale's new medical AI master's (below) uses the same idea with in-person bootcamps.

**Alumni-friendly online degrees.** Case Western gives its own alumni extra scholarship money on top of the online tuition reduction, and Maryland's residency-blind rate makes the MEng easy for its graduates to finish from anywhere. Florida Atlantic runs 4+1 BS/MS pathways into BME and AI, but these are on campus (second pass).

Inference: the "finish your master's remotely" idea is proven at Penn but absent in bioengineering nationally, which makes it a low-cost enrollment feeder BU could own for its own undergraduates.

### Residential medtech and biodesign programs

These aren't online competitors, but they show where new medtech investment is going, and all of it is in person. ASU's MS in Innovations in Medical and Patient Care Technologies (IMPACT) launched Fall 2025 with Mayo Clinic; it's a 30-credit, full-time, in-person program built on the Stanford Biodesign process, and graduates also earn a 3-credit Mayo clinical innovation certificate ([SBHSE](https://sbhse.engineering.asu.edu/degrees/graduate/); program details second pass). UC Davis's MEng in Medical Device Development enrolled its first cohort in September 2025: nine months, twelve courses, in person at Aggie Square, with a three-quarter capstone and clinical shadowing ([UC Davis](https://bme.ucdavis.edu/graduate/medical-device); launch details second pass). Keck Graduate Institute's MS in Medical Device Engineering is nine months, full time, and ends in an industry-sponsored team project ([KGI](https://www.kgi.edu/degrees-and-programs/biotechnology/master-of-science-in-medical-device-engineering/)). UC San Diego runs an MEng medical device specialization ([UCSD](https://bioengineering.ucsd.edu/grad/programs/me-mds)).

Northeastern deserves attention because it's in Boston and recruits from the same employers. Inference: an engineer at a Boston device company choosing between learning the regulatory side and a BME master's will see Northeastern first.

## 5. Market structure

### Who runs these programs

Most programs here are built in-house through a school-level online or professional unit: JHU EP, Columbia CVN, USC DEN, Maryland MAGE, Houston's UH-Extend, Purdue Online, and Northeastern CPS. Case Western is the only confirmed OPM relationship. ASU ended its decade-long Pearson contract in 2023 and brought the work in-house ([Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/tech-innovation/teaching-learning/2024/10/10/report-online-program-manager-growth-slows)).

The OPM sector has shrunk. 2U went through bankruptcy in 2024, and OPM revenue last year was about $3 billion against an earlier $8.25 billion projection ([Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/tech-innovation/artificial-intelligence/2026/06/01/will-ai-help-revive-stale-opm-market)). In one survey, only 10% of chief online officers at four-year schools were considering an OPM and 59% were not ([Encoura](https://www.encoura.org/resources/wake-up-call/reimagining-the-opm-market-a-human-centered-framework/)). Inference: BU Virtual's in-house model matches where the sector has gone, so production capacity, not revenue share, is the constraint to plan around.

### Enrollment trends

No verified five-year series exists for online engineering master's specifically. The nearest data: overall graduate enrollment fell 4.7% in 2022 ([Inside Higher Ed on CGS](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/admissions/graduate/2023/10/18/graduate-enrollment-slows-even-applications-surge)); international graduate numbers fell 2.7% in 2024–25 and 12% in the fall 2025 snapshot ([AACRAO](https://www.aacrao.org/news/2025-data-and-the-global-student-trajectory/)); and new international enrollment fell 17% that fall ([IIE](https://www.iie.org/blog/four-things-to-know-about-the-fall-2025-snapshot/)). Inference: campus professional master's that depend on international students are under pressure, and online programs for employed domestic engineers are the obvious hedge.

### Price bands

The range now runs from $22,350 (Houston) to about $89,000 (Columbia), in three bands. Public low-cost programs run $22,000 to $35,000 (Houston, Colorado State, Purdue resident). The middle runs $39,000 to $47,000 (Case Western, Purdue higher tier, Maryland). Private premium programs run $56,000 to $89,000 (JHU, USC, Columbia). List prices at privates are rising about 3% a year: JHU went from $5,455 to $5,620 per course ([JHU catalog](https://e-catalogue.jhu.edu/engineering/engineering-professionals/academic-policies/tuition-fees/)) and USC from $2,665 to $2,742 per unit. Inference: Case Western discounting every new online student reads as price competition. I haven't verified BU's graduate per-credit rate, so BU's placement is open.

### Who the students are

Evidence is thin. Michigan reports that 63% of its online graduate engineering students are employed at enrollment ([U.S. News](https://www.usnews.com/education/online-education/university-of-michigan-ann-arbor-OENG0193/engineering)). JHU EP tells employer-paid students to start paperwork early ([JHU EP](https://ep.jhu.edu/admissions-aid/tuition-fees/)), which implies sponsorship is common, but no share is given. Not sourced here: the federal tax-free cap on employer education benefits (IRS Section 127) is $5,250 a year. Inference: if employers anchor to that cap, a working engineer gets about one course a year paid at JHU or USC prices, which makes part-time pacing and a certificate entry point matter more than headline cost. How device companies actually structure tuition benefits is industry practice I can't verify.

## 6. Curricular white space and demand

### Demand evidence

BLS projects 8% growth for bioengineers and biomedical engineers from 2025 to 2035, about 1,200 openings a year, and a May 2025 median wage of $109,370 ([BLS OOH](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/architecture-and-engineering/biomedical-engineers.htm)). Its 2024–34 projections name remote patient monitoring and wearable sensors as R&D drivers for this occupation ([BLS MLR](https://www.bls.gov/opub/mlr/2026/article/industry-and-occupational-employment-projections-overview.htm)). Inference: that's a small occupation, and many device engineers are classified as mechanical, electrical, or software engineers, so BLS understates the reachable market.

The FDA's AI-enabled device list is the clearest subfield signal. It had 692 entries in October 2023 ([UW Radiology](https://rad.washington.edu/news/fda-publishes-list-of-ai-enabled-medical-devices/)), 882 by March 2024 ([Agarwal](https://naveenagarwalphd.substack.com/p/ai-in-medtech-insights-from-882-device-approvals)), 1,451 at the end of 2025 ([IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/fda-approved-ai-medical-devices-list)), and 1,524 by August 2026 ([Paragon](https://paragoninstitute.org/ai-tools/ai-medical-devices/)). Radiology is about three-quarters of it. The FDA says the list isn't exhaustive.

Job-posting data: not verified; no Lightcast access. Michigan cites Lightcast in its own launches ([Michigan OPE](https://ope.engin.umich.edu/?p=17422)), so ENG's GPO or BU enrollment may have a license. Professional society signals (RAPS, AAMI): not searched.

### What's hard to teach online

Wet-lab work is the hardest. JHU uses lab weekends, Case Western uses simulation software and joint teams with campus students, and Purdue simply offers no lab courses online. UM-Dearborn shows the failure mode: a hybrid label with no online core. Clinical immersion is next; programs that treat it as essential keep it residential (ASU with Mayo, UC Davis, KGI), and USC keeps its device degree off DEN. Clinical engineering needs hospital practicum; UConn puts the coursework online, and Marquette's program stopped admitting. Inference: hardware kits (sensor boards, microcontrollers) ship well and support wearables and signal work, while anything needing a machine shop, cleanroom, or animal model does not.

## 7. Where the gaps are

### What the expanded scan changed

The direct field is larger than the first pass found, about eleven programs rather than six, but still thin. Price pressure is stronger: Houston at $22,350 and Colorado State near $29,000 sit well below Purdue, so BU can't compete on cost. Purdue remains the main positioning threat because it already sells the device-and-regulatory combination. The AI-in-medicine space is filling faster than expected, through Michigan, Louisville, Yale, and Florida, though mostly from the data and algorithm side rather than device engineering.

### Genuine gaps

**Engineering and regulation of software and AI-enabled devices.** Still the strongest gap, but narrower than it looked. New AI-in-medicine degrees teach the algorithms; none found teaches software lifecycle, validation, cybersecurity, change control for learning algorithms, and the regulatory pathway together as device engineering. BU's edge is that regulatory and clinical layer, not the machine learning. How software validation actually runs inside a device company is tacit industry practice; your teaching covers the frameworks, and practitioner faculty would need to cover the rest. Michigan's Fall 2027 launch is the date to beat.

**Connected and wearable devices with remote monitoring.** Still only single courses across the field, named by BLS as a demand driver, and deliverable online with hardware kits. Whether BU BME faculty strength supports it isn't verified.

**A finish-remotely pathway for BU's own undergraduates.** Penn proved the model, and no bioengineering program offers it. It's a feeder rather than a curricular gap, but it's cheap and hard to copy quickly.

### Contested, not empty

**Device development for engineers.** Purdue owns it at roughly half private pricing, CSUN runs an online cohort, and regulatory degrees (Northeastern locally) own the non-engineer version. BE695 is a real asset, but this is a differentiation play against Purdue, built on design-controls teaching and the Boston device network.

### Gaps for good reason

Tissue engineering and biomaterials are thin online because the lab is the content. Clinical engineering and healthcare technology management need hospital practicum, and one joint program has stopped admitting. A standalone imaging master's is weak: USC's is unavailable and imaging demand is moving into AI devices. Computational biology and health data science under a BME label add little over existing informatics degrees (inference). Pure regulatory affairs is saturated, with Northeastern in BU's own market. Biodesign looks like an online gap, but immersion is the point, which is why ASU, UC Davis, and KGI all built it in person.
